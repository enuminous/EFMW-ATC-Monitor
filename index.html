Read the proposal ↗ Explore the documents ↓

The research question

Can recursive memory and provenance checks help engineers identify inconsistent system records, with useful warning time and evidence they can trace?

Proposed boundary: authorized copies of telemetry and logs, isolated from operational command paths.

Current status: four research documents. Monitor implementation, evaluation datasets, and experimental results are not included. Aviation efficacy and safety remain unvalidated.

02 / EVALUATION

Evidence before advancement

The proposed evaluation progresses through four stages. These are planned activities, not completed milestones.

  1. 0

    Protocol and governance

    Establish authorized data use, isolation, independent custody, and frozen scoring rules before a run.

    Review stage 0 ↗
  2. 1

    Historical replay

    Compare incident, recovery, and clean periods against existing alarms, thresholds, EWMA, and CUSUM where appropriate.

    Review stage 1 ↗
  3. 2

    Controlled simulation

    Evaluate delays, gaps, drift, failover, and mixed configurations, including benign anomalies and correlated noise.

    Review stage 2 ↗
  4. 3

    Optional shadow study

    Only after independent review, consider an FAA-authorized study using copied data and verified one-way isolation.

    Review stage 3 ↗

Measure detection, delay, nuisance alerts, evidence traceability, uncertainty, robustness, and reviewer usefulness. Preserve negative and inconclusive findings. Read the measures and decision gates.

03 / SCOPE

Isolated observation for engineering review

The proposed monitor would read approved copies of records and produce evidence-linked findings with uncertainty visible.

Read the complete safety boundary ↗
  • No authority to issue or alter clearances, routes, equipment commands, or ground-stop decisions.
  • Written data authorization, a named owner, access controls, and an audit trail.
  • Technical verification of isolation, independent security review, and predefined stop conditions.
  • Existing FAA procedures and personnel retain operational authority.
